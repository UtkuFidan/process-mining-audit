# Purchase-to-pay process audit with process mining

Data: BPI Challenge 2019 (doi:10.4121/uuid:d06aff4b-79f0-45e6-8ec8-e19730c248f1), CC BY 4.0. Scope analysed: full log, 251,734 purchase order items (cases), 1,595,828 events. Every figure below is generated by `pm-audit report`; nothing is hand-edited.

Source: the challenge's original CSV release (1,595,923 events, matching the official event count) from a public GitHub mirror, because the official 4TU/figshare download returned HTTP 504 when this analysis was run

Data note: 95 events carrying the placeholder date 1948-01-26 (vendor invoices and debit memos) were excluded.

## Summary

- The process has 13,878 distinct variants; the top 10 cover 58.9% of cases and 78.8% of variants occur exactly once (long tail).
- Median throughput is 64 days (P90 126, P95 142).
- Each item category is checked against its own normative model: 70.7% of the 237,236 modelled items replay without deviation (per category below).
- Largest control exceptions by count: C5 Payment later than the usual days-to-pay plus 14 days (30,792, 16.8%); C4b Price or quantity changed after goods receipt (5,768, 2.5%); C2 Maverick buying (PO created after the invoice) (3,621, 1.7%).

## Findings

BPI 2019 assigns each item to one of four categories, and the controls differ between them: an invoice ahead of the receipt is allowed by configuration on 'invoice before GR' items and a bypass on GR-based items, and consignment stock is settled without an invoice chain. ### Late payment against an empirical terms proxy

The log has no contractual payment terms, so a flat threshold such as 60 days would treat a vendor on 14-day terms and one on 90-day terms the same. Instead each item is compared with the median invoice-to-payment time of its own vendor, taken over all of that vendor's paid items. Vendors with fewer than 30 paid items use their company's median. An item is flagged when it is paid more than 14 days (about one payment-run cycle) later than that median. 471 vendors have their own proxy, covering 175,300 items; 7,969 items use the company median. The proxies run from 17 days (P10) through 42 (median) to 92 (P90). A vendor that is always paid late gets a late proxy, so the test finds departures from the vendor's usual behaviour, not contract breaches.

## Limitations

- Case = purchase order item; amounts are cumulative net worth, not invoice values.
- The log has no payment terms and no vendor master data. Late payment uses an empirical proxy (above), and a vendor-creator-also-pays test cannot be run.
- There is no explicit approval event. `Change Approval for Purchase Order` and `Release Purchase Order` are the closest, and they exist for only a few percent of items, so the approval-based tests cover a small population.
- `Record Service Entry Sheet` is always posted under a system user, so the receipt-side segregation test covers goods receipts only.
- Items still open when the log ends (April 2020) appear as missing steps in the conformance results. Timestamps are used as recorded.
- Events with identical timestamps keep their order in the source file; variant counts can differ slightly from other tools.
- Flags are exceptions for follow-up, not proof of error or fraud; many have legitimate causes (blanket orders, credit memos, partial deliveries).
